# Design brief

## Problem

At compiler b2e1f3d1e4ce0338d4c4662db9a1326f2e2cb899, Dict’s public generic-key model and behavior diverge from darklang/dark 04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d, despite a complete native HAMT implementation.

## Goals

- Expose the interpreter’s string-keyed, single-value-parameter Dict contract.
- Match construction, lookup, duplicate handling, traversal guarantees, equality, and language-visible failures.
- Preserve the native HAMT, runtime ownership model, and non-public helpers.
- Remove or explicitly document public compiler-only extensions and AOT-specific divergences.

## Proposed solution

Retain the compiler’s existing HAMT representation behind a parity-focused public boundary modeled on the pinned interpreter’s F# Dict contract. Restrict public Dict typing and operations to the interpreter-supported combinations, align construction and update semantics including duplicate-rejecting and overwriting paths, provide the same callable surface and result shapes, implement content-based equality and compatible traversal behavior, and reproduce observable failures at the earliest appropriate AOT boundary. Keep representation helpers private and treat the DCB1 report at 8a402797 only as historical evidence.

## Success criteria

- The same supported Dict programs expose matching type names, callable names, signatures, arities, and results on both pinned revisions.
- Duplicate keys, missing lookups, merges, updates, equality, and traversal-dependent operations exhibit the interpreter contract.
- Unsupported key, value, or generic combinations are rejected without weakening the HAMT implementation.
- No compiler-only public Dict aliases or operations remain unless documented as intentional AOT divergences.
- Existing native Dict correctness and ownership behavior remain intact.
