# Design brief

## Problem

At compiler revision 51093e0a8e31fe45a9aa79a317fbefd6b74fbcc3, floating-point and math behavior remains incomplete or observably different from interpreter revision 04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d, particularly across parsing, rounding, transcendental operations, and textual rendering.

## Goals

- Complete interpreter-compatible numeric contracts while explicitly retaining compiler-only extensions and documenting intentional divergences.
- Make edge-case behavior deterministic for signed zero, nonfinite values, parse failures, rounding boundaries, and domain errors.
- Attach revision-stamped source or same-source behavioral evidence to every retained parity finding.

## Proposed solution

Deliver one revision-stamped parity slice that treats the current interpreter’s public behavior as canonical, fills the remaining floating-point and transcendental surface, and replaces bounded decimal generation with shortest-roundtrip rendering that preserves required special-value behavior. Maintain an explicit classification of parity contracts, compiler extensions, and intentional divergences, keeping closely related numeric differences within the same coherent change.

## Success criteria

- Equivalent same-source programs produce matching observable results at the recorded compiler and interpreter revisions across the completed contracts and representative edge cases.
- Text conversion round-trips finite values and handles signed zero and nonfinite values consistently with the interpreter.
- Parse failures and rounding behavior expose the canonical public types and semantics.
- Compiler-only extensions remain available and are clearly distinguished from parity behavior.
- Each claimed gap or divergence has current revision-pinned source evidence and, where practical, a focused behavioral probe.

## Open questions

- What canonical behavior should upward integer rounding expose, given that the interpreter contract remains unresolved?
