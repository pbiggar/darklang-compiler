## Important changes

- **Candidate lowers eligible Int64 `2^k + 1` multiplies to canonical `Add_shifted` LIR** — **Reason:** ARM64 can emit one shifted-register ADD without a materialized shift temporary.
- **Candidate adds ARM64 symbolic/encoding, x64-equivalence, LIR snapshot, and overflowing E2E coverage** — **Reason:** prove instruction selection, encoding, wrapping behavior, and generated-code correctness.
- **Candidate documents the optimization in `docs/classic-optimizations.md`** — **Reason:** record eligibility and the intentional `2^k - 1` non-fusion boundary.

## How it works

The focused candidate workload was `collatz`, which exercises multiplication by three. The registered verifier completed one warmup plus three comparable samples per revision and proved normalized MIR, generated executable bytes, and execution output equal. Candidate timing was 0.7 ms median versus 0.5 ms baseline: a 0.2 ms regression, below the 1.0 ms clear-win threshold. No alternate implementation or workload was attempted because this was a preserved-candidate measurement only; the checkpoint remains available and no files were edited, replaced, discarded, or integrated.

## Algorithm

1. Detect adjacent Int64 constant-multiply patterns with an eligible `2^k + 1` constant.
2. Confirm the constant temporary is dead after the multiply.
3. Replace the constant move and multiply with unary `Add_shifted(destination, source, shift)`.
4. Allocate and lower that form to one ARM64 `ADD (shifted register)` using the source as both operands.
5. Lower the same LIR form on x64 as equivalent wrapping immediate multiplication.
6. Keep `2^k - 1` as shift plus subtraction because ARM64 shifted SUB cannot express the required reversed operand form in one instruction.
