# Design brief

## Problem

At compiler b2e1f3d1e4ce0338d4c4662db9a1326f2e2cb899, operator expressions can diverge from interpreter 04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d in accepted syntax, precedence, associativity, partial application, desugaring, and pipeline behavior.

## Goals

- Make interpreter public behavior the operator-grammar baseline
- Treat all operator positions and pipelines as one expression boundary
- Classify and remove compiler-only legacy behavior while identifying intentional divergences
- Preserve interpreter argument order, evaluation order, arity, and failure timing

## Proposed solution

Define one operator-expression contract from source-backed comparisons and focused same-source probes at the pinned revisions, using DCB1 report 8a402797 and current parity documents only as leads. Align parsing and desugaring across prefix, infix, parenthesized, sectioned, partially applied, and pipeline forms; migrate affected expressions before removing legacy tokens, precedence rules, rewrites, and aliases. Keep primitive invocation behavior within this boundary while leaving general comparison meaning to the comparability work.

## Success criteria

- Matched programs parse, associate, evaluate, and fail identically under the pinned compiler and interpreter revisions
- Partial application and pipelines preserve interpreter-visible argument placement and evaluation order
- Legacy compiler-only operator behavior is absent unless recorded as an intentional divergence
- Every retained parity finding is traceable to current source evidence and, where practical, a focused same-source probe
