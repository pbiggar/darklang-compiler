# Trial result

The candidate only reconciles documentation and showed no retained compiler/runtime optimization; SSA Construction was slower with equal MIR, executable, and output. No established-suite improvement was proven; overall multiplier: 1×.

## Evidence

## Problems

Establish one current, distinct edigits compiler/runtime candidate or retire the stale investigation claims.

## Solution

Candidate overlap was confirmed before measurement. The registered SSA Construction verification used the pinned arm64 baseline and candidate, current edigits source, MIR, generated assembly via -vv, executable fingerprints, and focused execution. Results were correct and byte/IR-equivalent, but the candidate median regressed from 295.9 ms to 389.6 ms; therefore no optimization is retained. Full tests and canonical routine benchmarks were intentionally not run because DCB owns those gates.

## Implementation

- Updated the edigits investigation to retain the checked-in per-digit SkewList baseline and retire stale packed/FingerTree claims.
- Made no compiler, runtime, benchmark-input, expected-output, or workload changes.

### Algorithm

1. Inventory the checked-in edigits workload and identify SSA Construction as the relevant pass.
2. Inspect the pass and rank repeated persistent-collection operations as the leading hypothesis.
3. Compare the pinned baseline and candidate in isolated worktrees using one warmup and three measured samples each.
4. Normalize MIR, compare executable bytes, and execute both generated programs for output equality.
5. Reject the candidate because its selected-pass timing did not improve beyond the noise threshold.

### Example

For benchmarks/problems/edigits/dark/main.dark, baseline SSA Construction samples were [289.5, 308.3, 295.9] ms with median 295.9 ms; candidate samples were [276.7, 389.6, 396.4] ms with median 389.6 ms. Normalized MIR, executable bytes, and execution output were equal, demonstrating documentation-only reconciliation rather than an implementation change.
