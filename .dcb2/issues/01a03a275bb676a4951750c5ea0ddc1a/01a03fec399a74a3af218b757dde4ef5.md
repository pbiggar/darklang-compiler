## How it works

The closed edigits graph establishes fresh List<Int64> origins, known direct calls, non-escape, and dead source bindings after replacement, but ANF arguments remain borrowed while callees execute. Reference-count insertion tracks alias obligations without establishing exclusive mutation rights, and the later self-tail ownership optimization cannot supply a consuming interprocedural contract early enough for representation selection. A Blob-backed fixture demonstrated that direct storage could eliminate SkewList traversal, path copying, and most reference-count operations, but selecting it would risk observable immutable-list aliasing, reference-count balance, and getAt/setAt failure behavior. The retained behavior is therefore the existing persistent List implementation with no added compiler complexity. Residual risk is confined to future work incorrectly equating last use with uniqueness; a sound retry requires explicit consuming parameter/return effects and allocation-extent proofs on every selected edge.

## Algorithm

1. Restrict consideration to values originating from edigits’ two fresh List<Int64> constructions. 2. Trace each value through the closed direct-call graph. 3. Reject any aggregate or closure storage, escape, indirect or unknown call, non-Int64 element, unmatched constructor, alias, or unproved ownership transfer. 4. Require an explicit consuming transfer for every selected parameter and return edge, plus allocation-extent proof preserving bounds failures. 5. Because the current borrowed-call IR cannot establish those requirements, retain persistent SkewList reads, path-copying updates, and existing reference counting unchanged.
