# Trial result

The direct-buffer lowering was conclusively rejected because edigits lacks a compiler-enforced consuming-call ownership proof, despite a 97.69% focused instruction reduction. Canonical routine benchmark: equal.

## Evidence

## Important changes

- **Defined the minimum ownership contract** — **Reason:** Exclusive mutation requires proven consuming transfers in addition to fresh origins, monomorphic Int64 elements, non-escape, and last use.
- **Rejected the guarded lowering** — **Reason:** ANF’s borrowed-call convention leaves simultaneous caller and callee ownership, making direct mutation unsound.
- **Retired superseded edigits candidates** — **Reason:** Array, unsafe-access, packed-digit, and generic-buffer proposals no longer belong in active investigation notes.
- **Recorded focused rejection evidence** — **Reason:** Preserve the transformation’s measured value and exact semantic blocker for future ownership work.

## How it works

The closed edigits graph proves distinct fresh List<Int64> origins, known direct calls, no aggregate or closure escape, and linear last uses. It cannot prove exclusive mutation because callers retain arguments across divideByN, addWithCarry, and addFactorialTerms. Reference-count analysis tracks alias obligations but does not grant mutation rights; post-RC self-tail ownership adoption is too narrow and too late for representation selection. A Blob-backed fixture demonstrated the desired performance and behavior, but manually assumes the missing transfer contract. Retaining such a lowering would risk immutable List aliasing, reference-count balance, and getAt/setAt failure behavior. No compiler implementation was retained; residual risk is limited to future work incorrectly treating last use as uniqueness.

## Algorithm

1. Restrict analysis to values originating from the two fresh edigits List<Int64> constructions.
2. Trace them through the closed direct-call graph and reject storage, closures, indirect or unknown calls, escapes, non-Int64 elements, and unmatched constructors.
3. Verify every updated binding is dead after its replacement-producing call.
4. Require an explicit consuming parameter/return transfer on every selected call edge and allocation-extent proof for bounds behavior.
5. Fall back to persistent SkewList lowering when any required fact is absent.
6. Reject the current candidate because step 4 cannot be established by the existing borrowed-call IR.
