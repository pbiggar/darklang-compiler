# Design brief

## Problem

Benchmark baselines are snapshots, not per-benchmark best-known counts: quick baselines require manual wholesale replacement, full verification rejects improvements, and recording can overwrite better counts with regressions.

## Goals

- Reject every instruction-count regression
- Accept improvements and advance each affected baseline independently
- Apply identical monotonic semantics to quick and full benchmarks

## Proposed solution

Treat each stored Dark instruction count as a per-benchmark performance high-water mark. Verification passes when a result is equal to or lower than its baseline and fails when higher. Recording updates only improved counts, preserves stronger existing counts for unchanged or slower results, and retains history separately. Require an explicit baseline reset only when a workload or measurement contract changes enough to invalidate comparison.

## Success criteria

- Faster results pass verification and become the new comparison baseline
- Slower results fail and cannot replace a better baseline
- Mixed runs advance improved benchmarks without weakening any others
- Quick and full benchmark workflows enforce the same monotonic policy
