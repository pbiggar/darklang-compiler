# Design brief

## Problem

Compiled programs lack parity with the interpreter’s polymorphic structural equality and explicit printing behavior, causing observable differences for supported values, opaque values, rendering, newlines, ordering, and output effects.

## Goals

- Match interpreter behavior for equality and printing at C@51093e0a8e31fe45a9aa79a317fbefd6b74fbcc3 against I@04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d.
- Cover every supported runtime value, including interpreter-defined handling of incomparable or opaque values.
- Keep portable behavior in the prelude and limit native support to structural equality and raw output primitives.
- Explicitly classify compiler-only extensions and intentional divergences, with performance excluded unless observable behavior changes.

## Proposed solution

Establish the pinned interpreter revision as the semantic baseline and introduce one coherent runtime-value semantics boundary: recursive structural comparison follows interpreter rules, while portable prelude compositions perform value rendering and printing variants over a minimal ordered output effect. Revalidate each retained parity gap against current source and focused same-source evidence, and document compiler-only value categories separately rather than treating them as parity behavior.

## Success criteria

- Pinned same-source comparisons produce identical equality results and identical incomparable or opaque outcomes for all shared supported values.
- Printing produces the same rendered bytes, newline behavior, sequence ordering, and observable output effects as the interpreter.
- Every claimed gap is traceable to current source evidence at the recorded revisions and, where practical, a focused probe.
- Compiler-only extensions and intentional divergences are explicitly identified and do not obscure interpreter parity.

## Open questions

- For runtime value categories that exist only in the compiler, which should receive defined equality or rendering semantics and which should remain explicitly opaque?
