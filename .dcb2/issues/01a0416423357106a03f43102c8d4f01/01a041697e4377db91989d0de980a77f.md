# Trial result

Rejected before implementation because current ANF loses the closed cumulative-table provenance needed for a sound non-benchmark-specific specialization.

## Evidence

## Problems

Remove generic skew-list lookup, Option allocation, and length traversal from fasta’s fixed 4- and 15-entry cumulative-table selections when current IR proves the tables are closed and finite.

## Solution

Baseline IR confirms the hot path, but `makeCumulative` receives runtime SkewList values after literal lowering; the optimizer has no effect/provenance fact proving that a particular `List<(Int64, Float)>` is the finite result of a pure literal call graph. Reconstructing that fact from raw list allocations or recognizing fasta function names would exceed the isolated issue and risk unsoundness. No unsafe specialization was retained.

## Implementation

- No source changes or commit retained.
- Pinned baseline inspected and bounded quick correctness check completed.

### Algorithm

1. Compile fasta quick with ANF and LIR dumps.
2. Trace the two literal tables through `makeCumulative` into `makeRandomFasta`.
3. Inspect post-RC selector lowering for lookup, Option construction, tuple retain/release, length, and recursive tail call.
4. Reject specialization because no closed-table provenance survives at the relevant ANF pass.

### Example

Before: `selectRandom(t372, r, idx)` executes `__digitsGetAt_tup2_i64_f64(t372, idx)`, constructs `(0, item)`, compares `r`, obtains `SkewList.length_tup2_i64_f64(t372)`, and tail-calls itself. A correct after form would be ordered `r < threshold` comparisons returning direct characters, but current IR cannot prove which thresholds belong to `t372` without new provenance analysis.
