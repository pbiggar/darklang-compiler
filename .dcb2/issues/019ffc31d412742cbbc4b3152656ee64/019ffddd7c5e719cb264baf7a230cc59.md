# Design brief

## Problem

At C@708e146d5ae74739332fa22b6bd2a7f1915dbc61, compiler-specific utilities and implementation hooks share one resolvable catalog and are loosely presented as parity, although I@04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d exposes none of that exact public surface.

## Goals

- Use current interpreter behavior as the parity baseline while labeling compiler extensions and intentional divergences explicitly.
- Give every compiler-only utility deliberate public or internal visibility.
- Preserve supported extension behavior while preventing normal source from resolving implementation-only facilities.
- Maintain revision-pinned source and focused-probe evidence without inferring compatibility from adjacent interpreter APIs.

## Proposed solution

Add explicit visibility and compatibility classification at the declaration and resolution boundary. Retain established utility and legacy module-local behavior as documented compiler-only public contracts; classify raw-memory and helper-only facilities as internal and admit them only while building trusted compiler/runtime code. Keep parity, public extensions, and intentional divergences as separate inventories, with module-local differences remaining in their existing parity domains.

## Success criteria

- Normal source resolves every deliberately public extension with unchanged observable behavior and documentation identifies it as compiler-specific rather than interpreter parity.
- Internal facilities remain usable by trusted compiler/runtime code but are unresolved from ordinary source.
- Every comparison records C@708e146d5ae74739332fa22b6bd2a7f1915dbc61 and I@04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d and is supported by current source evidence plus focused probes where practical.
- Parity records contain no compatibility claims based only on similarly purposed APIs elsewhere in the interpreter ecosystem.
