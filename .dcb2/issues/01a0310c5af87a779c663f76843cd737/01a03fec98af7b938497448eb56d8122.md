## How it works

The baseline dominator-only CSE cannot reuse a scalar expression available on just one join predecessor. The candidate chose conservative edge insertion plus a typed phi after rejecting broader speculation and floating-point motion. A structured source probe was already optimized earlier and therefore neutral; the direct MIR fixture demonstrates the intended operation-count reduction. On the registered edigits workload, however, the candidate median was 102.3 ms slower and variability made the difference smaller than the 172.8 ms clear-win threshold. The verifier consequently rejected it as neutral/noisy. Complexity increases through predecessor, dominator, definition, availability, and barrier analysis; residual risks include compile-time overhead and increased phi live ranges or copy pressure. The candidate remains available but is not supported for integration by this measurement.

## Algorithm

1. Build predecessor, immediate-dominator, and SSA-definition maps. 2. Inspect the first non-phi instruction at an acyclic join for an eligible explicitly typed scalar binary expression. 3. Require dedicated jump predecessors and operands that dominate every insertion point. 4. Scan predecessor exports while invalidating availability at calls, allocation, memory, reference-count, runtime-error, and other barriers. 5. Continue only when the expression is available on at least one but not all predecessors. 6. Insert the expression, preserving operand order, on missing predecessors. 7. Replace the join computation with a typed phi selecting existing or inserted results. 8. Rely on the existing optimizer fixed point for propagation, dead-code elimination, and CFG cleanup.
