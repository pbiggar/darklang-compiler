# Trial result

The focused pass median regressed from 6.1 ms to 12.6 ms (+6.5 ms, +106.6%), exceeding the 1.0 ms threshold, so the preserved candidate is not a clear win. No established-suite improvement was proven; overall multiplier: 1×.

## Evidence

## Problems

Specialize profitable direct call sites with non-uniform literal arguments without globally rewriting every caller.

## Solution

The candidate substitutes concrete Int64 or Bool arguments into bounded helper clones, allowing downstream folding to remove literal-controlled branches without globally rewriting callers. Focused tests establish convention preservation, exclusion rules, clone limits, native behavior, and deterministic generation. However, the exact specialization pass more than doubled its focused compile time, so the runtime branch-removal benefit does not satisfy the required clear-win contract. Residual risk is concentrated in compile-time overhead and code growth; the registered verifier also failed closed because its timing parser cannot parse the pass number `2.4.5`.

## Implementation

- Clones eligible nonrecursive direct-call-only helpers for two or three distinct scalar literals.
- Retargets matching normal, borrowed, and tail calls while retaining unmatched callers.
- Excludes escaping references, closures, indirect uses, managed literals, and recursive cycles.
- Bounds growth to three variants per function, eight per program, 192 cloned nodes, and 64 source nodes per callee.
- Adds focused ANF and E2E coverage and documents the optimization in `docs/classic-optimizations.md`.

### Algorithm

1. Run uniform direct-parameter propagation.
2. Recompute direct calls, escaping uses, function sizes, and the direct-call graph.
3. Reject recursive, escaping, indirect, closure, managed-literal, or oversized cases.
4. Select one foldable parameter with two or three distinct concrete Int64/Bool values.
5. Enforce per-function and program-wide clone/node budgets.
6. Clone the helper once per selected literal, remove that parameter, and substitute its value.
7. Retarget matching normal, borrowed, and tail calls without changing their convention.
8. Retain the original helper for unmatched callers; otherwise remove it.
9. Let downstream MIR/LIR optimization eliminate constant branches.

### Example

Before, `choose(mode, value)` receives `choose(1, n)` and `choose(2, n)` and its MIR contains `Cmp(mode, 1)` plus a branch. Afterward, calls target `choose$literal_0_1(n)` and `choose$literal_0_0(n)`; the two clone MIR bodies contain no mode comparison or conditional branch. Baseline and candidate still print the identical result, and repeated candidate compilation produces identical normalized MIR and executable bytes.
