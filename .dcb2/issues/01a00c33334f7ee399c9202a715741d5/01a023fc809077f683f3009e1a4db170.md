Yes—the published Pisum result is not credible. It is almost certainly a bad/stale benchmark measurement, not a genuine Dark win over Rust.

The audited Dark and Rust sources both perform 500 × 10,000 reciprocal-square iterations and share the expected output `1644834071848`; their parity hashes mark them comparable. Rust is compiled with `rustc -C opt-level=3`.

But running Cachegrind directly on the current Dark Pisum executable produces **55,014,671 instructions** and **5,001,019 branches**—exactly the branch scale expected for five million inner-loop iterations. Against the recorded Rust baseline of **50,258,602 instructions**, Dark is about **1.095×** Rust, not 0.00×.

The published Dark value, **95,305 instructions**, is **577× smaller** than the directly measured executable and its historical branch count is only 21. That cannot represent the declared workload. It indicates that the recorded baseline measured an artifact/path where the loop did not run (or otherwise recorded the wrong executable/result).

So: the benchmark source itself does not appear to be cheating, but the reported Pisum comparison is invalid and should not be used as evidence of performance superiority until the Dark routine snapshot is re-measured and its provenance investigated.
