# Design brief

## Problem

Pisum’s reported 0.00x Dark/Rust ratio is not a comparable runtime result: Dark hoists the effect-free, invariant inner sum out of the 500-round loop because only the identical final result is observed, executing roughly one 10,000-term sum; Rust preserves all 500 sums under its floating-point optimization model.

## Goals

- Make pisum measure repeated runtime reciprocal-square computation rather than a removable redundant repetition.
- Preserve equivalent numerical behavior and observable output across Dark and Rust.
- Ensure published performance ratios describe comparable work.

## Proposed solution

Redefine pisum’s workload so every round contributes a distinct, observable numerical result and cannot be replaced by one invariant inner-sum evaluation. Keep the benchmark’s numerical focus and equivalent algorithmic opportunity in both languages, then refresh its parity contract and recorded measurements. The current result should be treated as non-representative of runtime performance, not evidence that Dark executes the original workload hundreds of times faster.

## Success criteria

- Both implementations produce the same defined result for the revised workload.
- Dark’s instruction count scales with the intended repeated computation instead of being near process-startup cost.
- The resulting ratio can be explained as code-generation/runtime efficiency rather than elimination of most benchmark work.

## Open questions

- Should benchmark policy allow whole-program elimination of a fixed workload when it is semantically valid, or require benchmarks to encode their intended operation count observably?
