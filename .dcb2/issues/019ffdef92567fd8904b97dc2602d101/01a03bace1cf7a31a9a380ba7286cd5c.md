Not safely as one general rule.

The candidate is correctly narrow: it matches only `TInt64`, where its carried update `derived + a` has the same wrapping 64-bit behavior as recomputing `a*i+b`. Extending to fixed-width `i8/i16/i32/u8/u16/u32/u64` is plausible, but needs a separate proof and implementation: MIR constant folding explicitly truncates each operation to the operand width, while the candidate currently creates `TInt64` phis and `Int64Const` operands. The rewrite must preserve that per-operation narrowing, and unsigned counted-loop guards/overflow must be proven separately.

`Int` should remain excluded: it is arbitrary-precision rather than wrapping `Int64` arithmetic. `i128/u128` also need independent backend and semantics evidence, not a mechanical inclusion.

Floats should not be included. IEEE-754 rounding makes `fl(a*(i+1)+b)` generally differ from `fl(fl(a*i+b)+a)`; NaN, infinities, and signed zero add further observable cases. A safe float version would need restrictive exactness/range proofs that are unlikely to justify its complexity.

Recommendation: retain `TInt64` for this candidate. If pursued later, make fixed-width integer support a separate trial per width family with overflow and unsigned-termination MIR/E2E proofs. The candidate’s reported deterministic ratio (`0.999893`) is not a material benchmark win, and its own commit states the routine benchmark profile was not run, so it does not yet satisfy the issue’s retain-only-material-wins/full-verification bar.
