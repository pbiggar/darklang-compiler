## How it works

The baseline MIR confirms that permutation and count state remains monomorphic Int64 List data reconstructed through reversePrefixCollect, rotateLeftCollect, push, append, List.getAt, and SkewList.setAt. Direct take/drop/reverse calls named by the older issue text had already been replaced by collectors, but equivalent traversal and reconstruction remained. The retained candidate confines a Blob-backed representation to proven bounded, non-escaping fannkuch state; ordinary Lists and their structural sharing and identity are untouched. An equal n=8 artifact-only harness produced a clear wall-clock win, and cachegrind independently showed an 89.46% instruction reduction for n=4. Complexity changes from repeated persistent path/list reconstruction and allocation to O(n) contiguous initialization/copy plus O(k) in-place rotation or reversal with three bounded allocations. Residual risk is that Unique operations depend on a documented non-aliasing invariant rather than a linear type; the representation is therefore intentionally internal and workload-specific.

## Algorithm

1. Allocate bounded reference-counted buffers for the permutation, counters, and reusable scratch state. 2. Initialize permutation words to 0..n-1 and counter words to 1..n. 3. Copy the current permutation into scratch before flip counting. 4. Reverse scratch prefixes in place until the first word becomes zero. 5. Rotate the permutation prefix in place and decrement the corresponding counter. 6. Reset exhausted counters and continue until every permutation has been visited. 7. Release all three Blob-owned allocations normally when fannkuch returns; all other List values continue using SkewList.
