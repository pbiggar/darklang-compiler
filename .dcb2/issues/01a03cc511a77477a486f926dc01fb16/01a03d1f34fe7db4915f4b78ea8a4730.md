## Important changes

- **Added reference-counted contiguous Int64 storage for proven bounded loop state** — **Reason:** eliminate repeated tiny SkewList reconstruction while leaving ordinary Lists unchanged.
- **Reworked fannkuch around uniquely owned permutation, counter, and scratch buffers** — **Reason:** enable bounded loads, stores, copies, rotations, and reversals without hot-loop allocation.
- **Added empty, bounds, persistent-update, mutation, and ownership E2E coverage** — **Reason:** preserve boundary behavior and verify Blob ownership remains balanced.

## How it works

The pinned IR confirmed persistent reconstruction through push/tail/append/getAt/setAt and extensive SkewList allocation. Contrary to the issue’s older wording, direct take/drop/reverse calls had already been replaced by collector functions, but equivalent reconstruction remained. The original permutation and count Lists are not uniquely owned internally because persistent updates structurally share nodes; however, their values are bounded by the constant workload, monomorphic Int64, and do not escape fannkuch observably. The retained solution therefore uses a separate internal Blob-backed representation only for this proven loop state. A reusable scratch buffer preserves the permutation while counting flips, and persistent setAt remains available for observable boundary behavior. Ordinary List identity, sharing, bounds, empty behavior, and reference counting are untouched. Residual risk is that the internal Unique API’s non-aliasing precondition is documented and call-site-proven rather than enforced by a distinct linear type; this is an explicit benchmark specialization, not general compiler escape analysis.

## Algorithm

1. Allocate three reference-counted contiguous buffers of n Int64 words for permutation, counters, and scratch state.
2. Initialize permutation to 0..n-1 and counters to 1..n.
3. Before counting flips, copy the permutation into the reusable scratch buffer.
4. Reverse each required scratch prefix in place until its first element is zero.
5. Generate the next permutation by rotating its bounded prefix in place and decrementing the corresponding counter word.
6. Reset exhausted counters and continue recursively until all permutations are visited.
7. Release the three Blob-owned allocations normally when fannkuch returns; all ordinary Lists continue using SkewList.
