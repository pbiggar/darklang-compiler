# Design brief

## Problem

At compiler b2e1f3d1e4ce0338d4c4662db9a1326f2e2cb899, non-recursive bindings differ from interpreter 04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d in syntax, destructuring, duplicate handling, and scope boundaries.

## Goals

- Adopt interpreter binding and annotation syntax while removing compiler-only forms.
- Match lexical visibility, use-before-binding, destructuring, duplicate-name, and nested-shadowing behavior.
- Keep recursive availability out of scope and classify unavoidable AOT diagnostic differences explicitly.

## Proposed solution

Model every non-recursive binding as an atomic lexical transition: evaluate its value once in the incoming scope, validate and destructure using interpreter rules, and expose the completed bindings only to its continuation. Apply the same scope discipline through sequences, conditionals, matches, lambdas, and nested expressions; reject duplicate usable binders consistently; prevent bindings from escaping failed, skipped, or unselected regions; remove compiler-specific binding syntax; and migrate repository-authored Dark source to the interpreter form.

## Success criteria

- Pinned same-source comparisons agree on accepted syntax, results, shadowing, duplicate rejection, and observable failures.
- Bindings are unavailable in their own value expression unless supplied by an outer scope.
- Failed destructuring aborts before the continuation, while skipped branches and nested scopes expose no bindings outside their lexical region.
- Repository-authored Dark source contains no removed compiler-only binding forms.
- Every remaining difference is documented as either an AOT-only extension or an intentional divergence against the exact pinned revisions.
