# Design brief

## Problem

Wall-clock compiler timings are too noisy to distinguish small performance changes reliably.

## Goals

- Produce reproducible baseline-to-candidate compile-performance comparisons
- Reliably identify differences of 1% or greater
- Use compilation of the current runtime benchmark corpus as the representative workload

## Proposed solution

Use a pinned Linux environment and deterministic instruction-count instrumentation, analogous to Valgrind, as the canonical compile-time performance metric rather than wall-clock duration. Compile the same runtime benchmark corpus with baseline and candidate compiler builds, aggregate their measured work into one candidate-to-baseline ratio, and classify changes of at least 1% as faster or slower; wall-clock timing may remain informational.

## Success criteria

- Repeated measurements of an unchanged revision produce the same classification and effectively identical ratio
- Injected compile-work changes of 1% or greater are reliably detected in the expected direction
- Every result reports one corpus-level ratio and labels the candidate faster, slower, or unchanged

## Open questions

- Should the deterministic metric cover the entire compilation command or only compiler-owned work?
- Which instruction-counting mechanism can measure the compiler reliably without nondeterministic runtime or toolchain activity?
