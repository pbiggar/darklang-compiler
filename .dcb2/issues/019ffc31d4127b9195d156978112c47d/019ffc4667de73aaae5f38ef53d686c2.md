# Design brief

## Problem

The compiler’s integer family is incomplete and behaviorally inconsistent with the interpreter, especially for fixed-width and 128-bit values, checked conversions, and failure semantics.

## Goals

- Match interpreter-visible arithmetic, conversion, parsing, formatting, random, aggregation, bitwise, and error behavior across arbitrary-width and fixed-width integers.
- Introduce complete signed and unsigned 128-bit support with representations and primitives that preserve width, signedness, overflow, and failure contracts.
- Keep compiler-only compatibility extensions outside the parity surface and explicitly classify intentional divergences.
- Pin comparison evidence to compiler 51093e0a8e31fe45a9aa79a317fbefd6b74fbcc3 and interpreter 04fbe9dcc995c6188757d583e273cbd30a3e2d3d.

## Proposed solution

Build the family on shared width-aware numeric representations and primitives, then expose interpreter-compatible portable wrappers with canonical result and error behavior. Validate the complete declared surface through a revision-pinned parity matrix and focused same-source observations, while containing related API differences within this coherent effort and documenting extensions separately.

## Success criteria

- Every interpreter-declared integer operation and checked conversion is available for its applicable widths and signedness.
- Equivalent programs produce matching values and failures for boundaries, overflow, division by zero, invalid shifts, malformed input, invalid random bounds, and failed conversions.
- Signed and unsigned 128-bit values behave consistently across arithmetic, comparison, conversion, aggregation, formatting, parsing, bitwise operations, and shifts.
- Parity evidence records both exact revisions and distinguishes interpreter parity, compiler extensions, and intentional divergences.

## Open questions

- What canonical behavior should govern the currently unresolved signed 128-bit addition case?
