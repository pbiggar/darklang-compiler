The raw-helper concern is resolved: they are compiler-internal, not public callable extensions.

`Stdlib.buildModuleRegistry` contains the `__*` helpers, but ordinary type checking rejects internal identifiers before normal function lookup; `src/Tests/e2e/stdlib_internals.e2e:5` asserts `__raw_alloc(8)` fails with “Internal identifier not allowed in user code.” The direct registry lookup at `src/DarkCompiler/Stdlib.fs:279-280` is therefore used by embedded stdlib and internal e2e suites, including stream/refcount tests, rather than exposing an ordinary language contract.

That leaves three public-exposure rows for the aggregate inventory:

- legacy `Stdlib.File.*`;
- legacy `Stdlib.Path.tempDir` and `Path.combine`;
- `Stdlib.Random.int64`.

The first two are explicitly classified as non-canonical compiler extensions in `docs/cli-parity.md:63-74`. `Random.int64` is a public compiler extension but required internally by `List.dark`, `String.dark`, and `Uuid.dark`; its native effect must remain reachable through a non-public route if its public name is removed. No other raw intrinsic is a candidate for this issue.
