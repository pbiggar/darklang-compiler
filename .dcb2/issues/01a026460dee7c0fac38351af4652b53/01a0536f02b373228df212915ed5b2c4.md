The regression is runtime-code fallout from source migration, not parser execution. Parsing finishes before benchmarked binaries run.

The routine profile has one regression: `tinytemplate` rises from **179,543,416,420** to **256,144,727,508** instructions (**+42.7%**). Every other listed benchmark has identical instruction counts. That single outlier moves the aggregate ratio from **4.27x** to **4.35x**.

The candidate rewrites TinyTemplate substantially to interpreter-compatible source. Its hot paths changed from direct list-pattern matches such as `head :: tail` into repeated calls to `List.isEmpty`, `SkewList.headUnsafe`, and `SkewList.tail`. Those occur in instruction lookup, context-stack traversal, template lookup, collection lookup, and rendering—operations executed repeatedly for every rendered row and template instruction.

It also replaces the one-pass `escapeParts` traversal with five nested `String.replaceAll` passes. TinyTemplate formats many rendered strings, so this turns one traversal into repeated full-string scans and intermediate allocations.

The benchmark record therefore reflects those compatibility rewrites, especially the list-deconstruction and escaping changes, rather than any cost of having one parser. The candidate’s parity hashes were updated because the benchmark source/workload contract changed; the benchmark is no longer measuring byte-for-byte the prior Dark program.
