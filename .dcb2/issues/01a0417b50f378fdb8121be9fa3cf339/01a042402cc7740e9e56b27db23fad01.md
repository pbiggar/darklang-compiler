# Trial result

The preserved candidate removes the intended LIR reference-count traffic, but the required focused verifier rejects it because MIR and executable bytes differ and the measured pass time has no clear win. No established-suite improvement was proven; overall multiplier: 1×.

## Evidence

## Problems

Remove redundant reference-count traffic when freshly built tree children are immediately assembled into a recursive sum.

## Solution

The candidate transfers the fresh ownership returned by two self-recursive calls through their tuple into the returned tagged sum, eliminating redundant retain/release operations while preserving observed program output. However, the registered focused verifier found changed normalized MIR and executable bytes, so the mandated MIR/executable equality proof fails; Reference Count Insertion also measured 0.7ms slower at the median, below the 1.0ms clear-win threshold. ARM64-focused snapshot coverage passes, but x64 parity and recursive-destruction proof remain unestablished by the required evidence.

## Implementation

- Candidate adds a narrow Reference Count Insertion ownership-transfer case for distinct fresh self-recursive children assembled into an immediately returned recursive-sum payload.
- Candidate LIR snapshot removes two child increments, one tuple increment, and the corresponding three decrements in `binary_trees`-equivalent construction.
- No files were edited, replaced, discarded, or integrated during this measurement.

### Algorithm

1. Identify a tuple whose elements are distinct variables bound to fresh self-recursive call results.
2. Require that tuple to be the payload of an immediately returned tagged recursive sum.
3. Suppress the child and tuple ownership retains and the matching deferred releases.
4. Let the outer sum own the tuple, and the tuple own both fresh children directly.
5. Reject retention unless focused equivalence and timing evidence establish the change.

### Example

Baseline `makeTree` LIR calls `makeTree` twice, emits `RefCountInc` for both children, allocates/stores the tuple, increments that tuple, allocates/stores the tagged sum, then decrements tuple and both children. Candidate emits the same two allocations and stores but no longer emits those six reference-count operations. Static `binary_trees` LIR counts changed from HeapAlloc 6 / RefCountInc 6 / RefCountDec 8 to HeapAlloc 6 / RefCountInc 0 / RefCountDec 2; both executables printed `6553500`.
