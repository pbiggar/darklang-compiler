The comparison fully accounts for the remaining regression.

Post-register-allocation LIR shows identical hot-path allocation, refcount, and call counts in the pre-record compiler and the compact-record candidate. The difference is repeated field loads in record updates:

| Hot function | Old LIR | Candidate LIR | Extra per invocation |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `applyPair` | 100 instructions, 14 heap loads | 124 instructions, 26 heap loads | +24 instructions |
| `moveBody` | 39 instructions, 7 heap loads | 45 instructions, 10 heap loads | +6 instructions |

Each simulation step invokes `applyPair` 10 times and has five inlined `moveBody` bodies:

`10 × 24 + 5 × 6 = 270` extra instructions/step.

Over 500,000 steps, that is **135,000,000 instructions**—essentially the entire measured residual of **135,000,102** instructions between the old 4.82x result and candidate `f0985dab`’s 5.36x result.

Cause: compact records fixed the physical layout, but `RecordClone` lowering still reloads fields that were already loaded earlier in the same update. For example, `applyPair` computes with `left.x/y/z`, `left.vx/vy/vz`, and `left.mass`, then reloads parts of `left` again while assembling the cloned `Body`; the same happens for `right`. The old tuple-based update lowering retained and reused those projection values. Every extra heap load also requires a `GpToFp`, hence the precise +24 / +6 instruction deltas.

This is a narrow regression repair, not broad escape analysis: extend projection/value reuse to immutable `RecordGet` operations, preserving the existing evaluation and ownership rules. It should restore nbody to approximately the old **4.82x** result without changing nominal record semantics or allocation behavior.

The temporary historical checkout and diagnostic binaries were removed. Both the main worktree and retained candidate are clean; no repository files were changed.
