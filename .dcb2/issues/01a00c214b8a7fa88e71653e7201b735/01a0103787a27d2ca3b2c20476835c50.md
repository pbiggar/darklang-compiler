### Finding

The `nbody` change is a known, deliberately accepted cost of the new nominal record representation—not a change to the benchmark workload and not a later JSON-specific regression.

| Snapshot | Compiler | Instructions | Data refs | Dark/Rust |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Pre-regression | `3a755ce9` | 1,193,502,272 | 663,000,662 | 4.82x |
| Current snapshot | `0d8fa52f` | 1,394,002,414 | 749,500,748 | 5.63x |

That is **+200,500,142 instructions (+16.799%)**, **+86,500,086 data references**, and **no branch-count change**. Per 500,000 simulation step, it is almost exactly **+401 instructions and +173 data refs**.

The benchmark source and workload contract are unchanged across `3a755ce9..0d8fa52f`; both snapshots use workload contract `3662376c…`.

### Cause

The causal change is `65ac0e4b67` — **Implement nominal interpreter-compatible records**. Its commit message explicitly records that the benchmark verifier rejected the run because `nbody` regressed by **+16.799%**.

Previously, records lowered through tuple-like allocation. The new representation uses distinct `RecordAlloc`, `RecordClone`, and `RecordGet` operations with this layout:

- slot 0: nominal descriptor identity;
- slots 1..N: fields;
- allocator reference-count trailer.

Consequently, a seven-field `Body` grows from a 56-byte payload to 64 bytes, and `System` grows from 40 to 48 bytes. Current post-allocation LIR shows that each simulation step allocates **26 nominal records**: 20 updated `Body` values from the ten `applyPair` calls, five moved `Body` values, and one replacement `System`. Every allocation stores its descriptor identity.

On ARM64, these descriptor identities have four nonzero 16-bit chunks, so each identity store expands to four `MOVZ`/`MOVK` instructions plus one store. That alone accounts for at least **130 instructions and 26 data refs per step**; the larger layout and resulting code/register-allocation effects account for the remainder.

The old baseline was retained while the regression was rejected. It was reset only later by `8386a891229` — **Reconcile routine baseline after record integration**—after the nominal record representation was approved.

### Recommendation

Do not simply revert the record representation: that would undo interpreter-compatible nominal record semantics. A narrow descriptor-hoisting or literal-pool optimization would reduce the cost but would not recover the full 16.8% regression.

The appropriate performance direction is already documented in `docs/investigations/benchmark-nbody-optimization.md`: scalar replacement/escape analysis for fixed-shape records across the unrolled pair updates. That would eliminate most intermediate `Body`, tuple, and `System` allocations rather than merely making their descriptors cheaper.

No repository changes were made, and I did not rerun the routine benchmark profile.
