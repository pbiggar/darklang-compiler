The candidate recovered **65,500,040 instructions** (4.70%), but **135,000,102 instructions remain** above the pre-record baseline: **+11.31%**, or almost exactly **270 instructions per simulation step**.

That is consistent with the tag removal addressing only one component. `nbody` creates 26 records per step; the removed descriptor costs roughly five ARM64 instructions per allocation, or about **130 instructions/step**. The candidate’s measured recovery is therefore the expected descriptor-store saving, plus small fixed effects. It does not restore the other ~270 instructions/step introduced with nominal-record lowering.

The remaining cost is not a necessary consequence of nominal typing, nor of the now-removed payload word. The old compiler lowered record construction, update, and access through generic `TupleAlloc`/`TupleGet`; the new compiler preserves distinct `RecordAlloc`/`RecordClone`/`RecordGet` operations throughout ANF and reference-count insertion. Although their compact final layout is now tuple-like, they no longer necessarily receive every tuple-specific simplification and can produce different ownership/liveness and register-allocation decisions.

The current repository evidence establishes two additional facts:

- All five `moveBody` calls are already inlined; there is no remaining direct-call optimization to recover.
- The hot loop still materializes the immutable intermediate `Body` values, pair-result tuples, and replacement `System`. The durable opportunity remains scalar replacement / escape analysis across `advanceStep`’s unrolled pair updates.

I cannot yet attribute the remaining 135M instructions to one exact instruction sequence from repository history alone—the candidate did not retain a pre/post LIR or Cachegrind data-reference comparison for the compact layout. The next investigation should diff normalized post-register-allocation LIR for `3a755ce9` and `f0985dab`, then classify the extra ~270 instructions/step into record projections/clones, reference-count operations, spills/save-restores, and tuple materialization. That would tell us whether a narrow regression repair exists before pursuing broader scalar replacement.
